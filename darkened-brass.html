02 / PLAIN-ENGLISH CONCEPTS

Why the notes and waves never clash.

Two music-math ideas behind every sound: a 5-note pentatonic garden and overtone-matched waveforms.

PENTATONIC SCALE · “PENTA” = 5 NOTES

Removing F and B makes every random combo work.

A regular 7-note major scale (C D E F G A B) has tight half-step rubs (E–F, B–C) and the tense F–B tritone. Dropping Fa (F) and Ti (B) leaves five open notes (C, D, E, G, A + high C) — just like only touching the black keys on a piano — so random jumps and overlapping 3-second reverb always blend cleanly.

OVERTONES · WHY 'TRIANGLE' + 'SINE' FIT REVERB

Different waves carry different hidden "bonus pitches."

Real instruments radiate a fundamental pitch plus quieter overtones (2×, 3×, 4×, 5×…). Pairing a soft 'triangle' lead with a pure 'sine' bass separates both voices clearly without muddying the 3-second reverb. Even the pentatonic ratios (2:1, 3:2, 5:4) come from simple overtone fractions.

In one sentence: triangle overtones make the melody step forward warmly, while zero-overtone sine keeps the bass underneath clean and glass-bell chimes clear.

1 · REVERB
reverb = new p5.Reverb(3); reverb.wet(0.5);

3-second hall echo at a 50% dry / 50% echo mix. Disconnect dry wires first so raw beeps don't bypass the room.

2 · LERP() SPEED
lerp(0.36, 0.14, chaos)

Smooth wait-time slider between notes: 0.36s per note when calm (chaos=0), speeding down to 0.14s at peak (chaos=1).

3 · !wasRest GUARD
if (!wasRest && random(1) < 0.22)

1-bit switch (“did we just pause?”). !wasRest prevents two rests in a row, capping silences under 0.4s.

4 · CHAOS BLEND
if (random(1) < chaos) r = random(ratios); else { t += 0.3; r = noise(t); }

Turns chaos into a percentage chance: gentle stepwise noise() early/late, unpredictable jumps at 30s.

5 · CHIME FLIP
chaos > 0.65 && random(1) < 0.35 ? 2 : 0.5

IF inside 18.6s–41.4s peak AND 35% roll hits → high 2× glass chime; ELSE stay deep 0.5× bass.
